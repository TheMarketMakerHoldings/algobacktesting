How to read your result

It judges your record, not your future

A high number means your past results are hard to explain by luck. Markets change, and an edge that was real can stop working.

Every setup you tried counts

Test five ideas and keep the best, and the best one looks good even if none of them works. Each extra idea raises the bar your results have to clear.

More trades prove more than a better month

Thirty good trades prove very little. The chart shows how many trades at your current results would settle the question.

The math behind it

The check measures how far your average result stands above zero, relative to how much your trades swing, and how unusual that would be for someone with no edge.

Then it asks what the best of several no-edge setups would look like, and holds your results to that standard instead.

The method is the Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe Ratio of Bailey and López de Prado (2012, 2014). This page assumes the setups you tried were independent and tested on a similar number of trades. Treat results from fewer than 30 trades as rough.

We tested it on simulated traders with no edge at all. With one setup it wrongly called the edge proven about 5% of the time, as intended. With five or more setups it did so about 1% of the time or less.

For developers

The same checks, plus overfitting and cross-validation tools for backtests, are in an open-source Python library. Every worked example in the source papers reproduces, and this page's calculator is tested against the library.

pip install algobacktesting

Available on PyPI from version 0.1.0. Requires Python 3.11 or later and NumPy.
