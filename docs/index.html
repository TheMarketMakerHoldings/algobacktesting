Is your trading edge real, or luck?

Import one strategy's trades from TradingView, TopstepX, Quantower, NinjaTrader or thinkorswim. The check holds your results to what the luckiest of the strategies you tried would have made with no edge at all, using the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014).

It runs in your browser. Your files are never uploaded, there is no sign-up, and there is nothing to buy.

      How it works

      The method, worked on the example

      Four steps, with the numbers the page gets for its made-up example file (its ORB strategy, – setups tried). They are worked out live by the same code that checks your trades.

      1. Measure the edge per trade

        The example's – trades average – after fees, and swing by – from trade to trade (one standard deviation). The edge per trade is the average over the swing: a Sharpe ratio of – per trade.

      2. Ask what luck alone would show

        Try – setups with no edge at all and keep the best, and the best still shows a Sharpe ratio of about – per trade, by luck (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014, equation 2). On these trades' swings that is – a trade.

      3. Compare, allowing for uncertainty

        The Deflated Sharpe Ratio is the chance the true edge beats that luck bar, given how many trades there are and how lopsided and fat-tailed the results are. For the example it is –.

      4. Say how much more proof is needed

        If future trades look like these, the chance would reach 95% at about – trades. Fees are always subtracted first, and breakevens count as the small losses they are after commission.

      The method is the Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe Ratio of Bailey and López de Prado (2012, 2014). It assumes the setups were independent and tested on a similar number of trades. Tested on simulated traders with no edge, it wrongly called an edge proven about 5% of the time for one setup, and about 1% or less for five or more.

      Export help

      Getting your trades out of your platform

      Export the closed trades of one account, then pick one strategy in the check. Menus change between versions: if yours look different, look for Export on the trades or history view.

      TradingView

      In the Strategy Tester, open List of trades and use the export button to download a CSV. Each trade has an entry and an exit row; the page counts each trade once, using its Net P&L, which already includes the commission set in the strategy's properties.

      TopstepX

      Open the Trades tab at the bottom of the platform (not Orders), choose the date range and click Export. If the file has separate fee columns, keep subtract fees on.

      Quantower

      Open the Trades panel, choose Export data from its menu, tick all columns, pick comma-separated and export. Fills that share a position ID are combined into one trade, using the Net P/L column. For the stop lab, export bars from the History exporter.

      NinjaTrader 8

      Control Center, New → Trade Performance, choose the account, strategy and dates, click Generate, open the Trades view, then right-click and Export. Check whether your Profit column already includes commission, and set subtract fees to match.

      thinkorswim (Schwab)

      Monitor → Account Statement, set the dates, then Export to File from the menu at the top right. Buys and sells in Account Trade History are paired into round trips, first in, first out. Commissions are not in that section, so results are before fees. The stop lab replays each round trip from the fill that opened it.

      Questions

      What the numbers mean

      Short answers to what traders ask most about this page.

      What does "chance the edge is real" mean?

      How sure you can be that your strategy makes money for a real reason, not because of a lucky streak. It looks at how many trades you have, how much your results swing from trade to trade, and how many setups you tried. At 95% or more, luck is a poor explanation for your results. Below 50%, your results are no better than the luckiest of your setups would typically show with no edge at all. It describes the past; it does not promise the future.

      What is a "setup", and why does the number matter?

      A setup is any strategy, indicator or set of rules you tested. Try ten ideas and keep the best, and the best one usually looks good even if none of them works: that is luck you selected for. So the check asks what the best of your setups would have shown with no edge at all, and holds your result to that bar. Count every version you tried and dropped. If this is the only idea you ever tested, enter 1.

      What is the dashed "luck alone" line?

      What the luckiest of your setups would typically have made by now if none of them had any edge. With one setup it sits at $0, because a strategy with no edge averages nothing. Each extra setup you tried raises it. Finishing above it is a good sign; the chance at the top says whether you are far enough above.

      Why are breakevens counted apart, and why do they cost money?

      A trade that was flat before fees still paid commission, so after fees it is a small loss. The page counts these trades apart from real wins and losses, shows your win rate with and without them, and shows what they cost you in total.

      Which time zone are the hours in?

      Your file's, exactly as written in it; the page cannot know which zone that is. TradingView exports in the time zone set on your chart (the clock at its bottom right). Most other platforms use the time zone set in the platform or on your computer.

      Why didn't my TradingView trades line up with my bars?

      Usually the two files cover different dates: load bars for the same months as your trades. Continuous contracts such as NQ1! are also adjusted at each contract roll, so their prices sit a fixed number of points away from real prices, and trades may be stamped one chart bar early. The stop lab finds and corrects both, and tells you what it changed.

      Is my data uploaded anywhere?

      No. Your files are read in your browser, and the page is locked so it cannot send data to any other site. Saved strategies stay in your browser on that device, and a shared link holds only summary numbers, never your trades.

      Is this financial advice?

      No. It is statistics about past results. A real edge can stop working when the market changes, and fills in live trading can be worse than in a replay.
